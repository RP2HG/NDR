## A history of false starts

This piece on [Not Programming, but Programming](../02 not programming.md) records my views on the challenges of using modern compute tools to create content. My feeling is that this is the main reason why I have had so many starts at creating this site, only to back away and start again. So below a short history of attempts at creating this site.


### General Tes





### Entering the WordPress Era

Personally, I am not an early adaptor of most technologies, and my adoption of WordPress is no exception. Our ISP had successively increased our broadband speed on regular occasions, and I had embarked on my MSc through the [Open University](https://www.open.ac.uk/). The course featured extensive use of the online forum for discussion purposes, but several of my cohort were also expressing themselves via blogging, and the blogging tool of choice was [WordPress](https://wibni.wordpress.com/).In fact, our course [Systems Thinking in Practice](https://www.open.ac.uk/postgraduate/qualifications/f47/) actively encouraged it.

And so it was that I followed several of my colleagues into creating content via WordPress. My piece about [Removing Constraints in Computing](../../Relflecting/01 wherehypecomesfrom.md) mainly comments about hype cycles, but I would argue that the history of products like WordPress follow a similar evolutionary path determined by the lifting of previous technology constraints.

As internet browsers expanded their capability through the advent of javascript and extensions to the HTML specification, assisted by ever improving bandwidth to carry larger web page payloads, so the  power of products like WordPress expanded to offer better writing and drafting support. 

### Wordpress Develops

I would say that my history with WordPress has been a classic _"It isn't you ... it's me"_. Follow the link to my site, and you'll see that my posting was very sporadic, and so it turned out that many times when I went to add an article that the WordPress structure and UI had changed, reflecting teh ever growing capabilities of web technologies. I found I was relearning WordPress often even though the added features were mainly accretive, it was the layout changes and drafting tool differences that I found dicerted my limited attention away from writing.

Along the way I did try using the desktop clients, but they too seeemed to be quite fluid.

### Going our separate ways

As you could see if you follow the links, I haven't deleted my WordPress site, but I haven't updated it recently. For anyone wanting to create a web site, I still think that WordPress has lots going for it, especially teh ability to quickly theme the site
