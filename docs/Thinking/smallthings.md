## Unix days

As a slow thinker it takes me a while, and a lot of reflection, to work many things out. Almost 50 years ago I started at University and met the Unix operating system then running, at my university computing debarment at least, on a 16-bit [PDP-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDP-11).[^1} Whilst using it at the time, I didn't appreciate until many years later when working on porting Unix System 5 how the use of small, specific piece of software, and having the ability to join them together in differing ways to provide powerful tools worked so well.

Compare this to other operating systems at the time, for example [VAX VMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAX#Operating_systems) as it was celled in teh eraly days, where an operating system utility could be hugely complicated with many parameters. One command-line instruction could be very powerful, but rather more stand-alone, a typical example of what I describe in my post about[bloatware in software creating architectural problems](../Relflecting/bloatware.md)

These days the flavour is Linux, and MacOS too, but that software architecture pattern of creating small, focused functional pieces of software, and joining them together stands today, and is still a good way ti create accretive functionality


## Agentic AI

I've grumbled [elsewhere](../Relflecting/AIgrmbles.md) about AI in general, but the promise of Ai agents as small units of functionality being pulled together to create exponentially more powerful solutions to our various needs is, on face value, a way to greater usability
